# The purpose this study serves

The study has one question: where the deterministic layer does not cover a failure mode, which model catches it, and in how many of n takes?

The record must show what happened rather than what would look good.
The first round earned its credibility from takes it published as failures and from predictions it made in advance and got wrong.
This round exists because reading the first round's transcripts after its results existed showed four places where the instrument measured something other than the property.
Fixing an instrument after the numbers exist is choosing the rules after seeing the data, so this round fixes it first, in writing, and shows each fix on the first round's own transcripts before anything is frozen.

The system under test argues that navigation needs a small brain: deterministic tools, contracts as the system prompt, refusals enforced below the model.
If that is right, the model matters least where the contracts are strongest and most where they are silent.
Whether a cheaper model holds the cells a more expensive one holds is the question, and either answer, published as counts, serves it.
A table that flatters the hypothesis serves nothing, and so does an instrument that flatters a model.

A criterion that stops serving an honest record gets flagged, never silently optimised.
The adversarial reader's first lens is this page.
